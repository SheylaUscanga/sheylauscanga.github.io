Artificial Intelligence ∩ Epistemic Agency
AI Travel Planners and Epistemic Agency → Current research interest

This project examines how AI-generated recommendations can shape a person's ability to understand their circumstances, evaluate alternatives, and decide what is possible for them. I am particularly interested in travel-planning systems in Mexico, where a recommendation may be factually accurate while still relying on assumptions about infrastructure, security, mobility, leisure, and everyday life that do not adequately reflect the context in which a user is making a decision. The project asks how these contextual assumptions affect the space of possibilities presented to users and, in turn, their epistemic agency.

Artificial Intelligence ∩ Regulation & Law
Configuring AI Decision-Making Capacity in the European Union → Undergraduate thesis · BUAP · 2016–2024

This thesis examines how the decision-making capacity of artificial intelligence is configured through the European Union's regulatory ecosystem between 2016 and 2024. It traces the development of the normative and institutional framework surrounding AI, from early data-protection and biometric provisions to the emergence of the AI Act and the institutional structures that accompany it. The project focuses on how European rules define, delimit, and organize the field of action available to AI systems, and on the relationship between technological capacities and the legal structures through which they are regulated.

Epistemic Agency ∩ Institutions & Power
Automation, Subjectivity & Critical Theory → Research seminar · BUAP

This project approaches artificial intelligence through critical theories of political economy, subjectivity, and power. It considers how increasingly automated systems participate in the organization of social relations and how technological forms can influence the ways individuals understand themselves, others, and the structures in which they are situated. Drawing on critical theory, psychoanalysis, and the Frankfurt School, the project explores the relationship between automation, subjectivity, and contemporary forms of domination without treating technological systems as independent from the social relations in which they are developed and used.

Regulation & Law ∩ Institutions & Power
AI Regulation and Institutional Configuration in the European Union → Research focus

This line of work examines AI regulation as an institutional process rather than as a collection of isolated legal provisions. It considers how European institutions, Member State priorities, regulatory instruments, and emerging governance structures interact in defining the conditions under which artificial intelligence can be developed and deployed. The broader question is how legal rules become part of the institutional architecture through which technological possibilities are delimited and organized.

Institutions & Power ∩ International Affairs
Cross-Border Cyber Defense → COIL research project · Mexico & Argentina

This comparative project examines national approaches to cybersecurity through the institutional and geopolitical contexts in which they are developed. Working across Mexico and Argentina, the project considers how states frame cyber defense, organize institutional responsibilities, and respond to a field in which technological infrastructures and political interests extend beyond national borders. The project connects questions of cybersecurity with broader concerns about international cooperation, state capacity, and the changing relationship between technology and international affairs.
